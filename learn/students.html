Lesson 01 · ~10 min

Welcome

AI can write your essay, solve your problem set, and summarize your reading, which is precisely the problem: everything it does for you is something you did not learn to do. The skill this course teaches is using the same power the other way around, so that AI makes you more capable instead of less.

The 4Ds apply here as everywhere (see Framework & Foundations): decide what belongs with AI, describe well, judge what comes back, and take responsibility. For students, one rule sits on top of all four.

The standing rule

You must be able to explain and apply everything you submit, even if AI helped. If you cannot, the work is not yours yet, whatever the file name says.

Lesson 02 · ~15 min

AI as a learning partner

The framing that changes everything: AI should act as a coach or tutor, not a substitute player. A coach makes you run the drill; a substitute runs it instead of you and leaves you exactly as unfit as before.

In practice, the strongest learning uses of AI are the ones where it challenges you: a debate partner that argues against your position, a tutor that asks rather than answers, an examiner that probes what you think you know. The verification test for any study session: can you explain it to someone else afterward? If not, you reviewed; you did not learn.

Key takeaways
  • Coach, not substitute player.
  • The test of learning is being able to explain it to someone else.
Lesson 03 · ~25 min

The study-buddy protocols

Configure your AI study partner once and reuse it. The base configuration, five standing instructions:

  1. Always make sure you understand my goal first.
  2. Ask me questions rather than giving me answers.
  3. Check my understanding before advancing.
  4. Give me practice problems.
  5. Connect new material to what I have already learned.

Per-mode protocols

  • Problem-solving: "Guide me with hints. Do not solve it."
  • Concept review: "Ask me progressively harder questions."
  • Exam prep: "Quiz me, and explain why the wrong answers are wrong."
  • Writing: "Help me develop my own arguments through questioning."
  • Reading: "Ask me to explain the key concepts in my own words."
Try it, and watch for the slip

Run the configuration on real coursework this week. Watch for the moment it slips into just giving answers, because it will; trained helpfulness pulls that way. When it slips, remind it of the protocol. Save the whole configuration as a reusable note you paste into new conversations.

Check yourself

You asked for hints; the AI solved the whole problem anyway. What do you do?

The slip toward answer-giving is expected: trained helpfulness pulls that way. Correcting it is part of running the protocol, and the struggle it preserves is the learning.

Key takeaways
  • Five standing instructions plus a per-mode protocol; save and reuse.
  • Expect the slip toward answer-giving and correct it.
Lesson 04 · ~20 min

Career planning with AI

A blunt fact first: generic AI-written application materials read as generic, and that is what costs you. The division of labor that works: AI does research, brainstorming, and interview practice; you drive the strategy and authentically represent yourself.

Used well, AI is a genuinely good career-reflection partner. Ask it to interview you with the questions that matter: What energizes you, and what drains you? Which trade-offs would you actually accept? What does values alignment look like in a workplace? What is your definition of success beyond job titles?

AI fluency is itself a career skill worth developing and showcasing. Being the person who works well with AI, and can say how, is a differentiator in nearly every field you might enter.

Key takeaways
  • AI researches and rehearses; you strategize and represent yourself.
  • Fluency itself is a career asset; make it visible.
Lesson 05 · ~25 min

Your personal AI policy

Write a short personal AI collaboration policy with four parts:

  1. Red lines. Situations where you absolutely will not use AI, named in advance so the decision is already made when the temptation arrives.
  2. Transparency commitments. When and how you disclose AI's role in your work.
  3. Skill-sharpness strategies. The abilities you will deliberately keep exercising without AI, so they stay yours.
  4. The reflection loop. Two questions, revisited monthly: is this helping me grow, and am I being honest about AI's role?
The empathy flip

Before finalizing, consider how you would want your classmates, your professors, and one day your employees to use AI, and which uses would make you uncomfortable if others did them. Anything on that list belongs on your own red-line list too.

Close with the claim this whole edition rests on: your specific talents, experience and perspective remain the part no tool supplies.

Key takeaways
  • Red lines, transparency, skill-sharpness, reflection loop.
  • The empathy flip finds the red lines you did not want to admit.
